# Resume Style Guide v2

This file is the working house style for the Custom GPT Knowledge shelf. It is based on the QS top-10 official undergraduate resume/CV guidance synthesis you provided and is written to be directly usable by the GPT during drafting and rewriting.

## 1. What a strong undergraduate resume is

Across top schools, the most consistent view is that a resume is not a full life record. It is a concise, evidence-driven marketing document meant to secure an interview.

It should:
- show the most relevant evidence, not every experience;
- be easy to skim quickly;
- translate the user’s background into the employer’s language;
- prioritize credibility over ornament.

Useful mental models across schools include:
- marketing piece;
- movie trailer;
- concise summary;
- marketing document;
- marketing tool.

## 2. Core principles

### 2.1 Tailoring is the first principle
A resume should be built against the role’s core requirements, not against the user’s desire to list everything.

The workflow is:
1. identify the target role and region;
2. extract repeated keywords, verbs, and required competencies from the JD;
3. map each requirement to the strongest available evidence;
4. reorder sections and bullets by relevance;
5. rewrite using employer language without changing facts.

If a document could be sent unchanged to many different roles, it is probably not tailored enough.

### 2.2 Evidence beats description
The strongest bullets do not merely state responsibilities. They show:
- what the user did;
- in what context;
- how they did it;
- what came out of it.

A useful default formula is:
**Action Verb → Task / Context → Method / Tool / Scope → Result / Impact / Reason**

### 2.3 Results matter, but truth matters more
Top schools consistently favor outcome-oriented bullets over duty lists. However, result language should never outrun the actual evidence.

Use numbers, percentages, scale, scope, time, frequency, volume, or output only when the source material supports them.

When the user has no quantifiable result, a bullet may still be strong if it clearly states the purpose, contribution, deliverable, or reason the work mattered.

### 2.4 Soft skills should be shown, not merely listed
Communication, teamwork, leadership, initiative, strategy, and judgment should be demonstrated through experience bullets, not dumped into a skills list without proof.

### 2.5 Experience is broader than internships
For undergraduate recruiting, valid evidence may come from:
- internships;
- part-time jobs;
- research;
- class projects;
- competitions;
- personal projects;
- student leadership;
- volunteering;
- community activity;
- exchange or co-curricular work.

## 3. Default global baseline

When no target region is specified, default to:
- undergraduate job or internship resume;
- 1 page;
- reverse chronological order within sections;
- ATS-safe, simple layout;
- strong relevance filtering;
- professional, specific, fact-based tone.

Default section logic:
1. Header
2. Education
3. Relevant Experience
4. Projects / Research / Leadership / Volunteering as needed
5. Skills
6. Awards / Additional Experience only if they add value

## 4. Regional rules

### 4.1 USA
Default label: Resume.

Rules:
- strongly prefer 1 page;
- prioritize ATS compatibility and fast skimming;
- do not include photo, DOB, age, marital status, nationality, religion, gender, sexual orientation, references, or full home address;
- keep layout simple and machine-readable;
- GPA only when it helps;
- coursework only when relevant;
- summary/objective only when specific and useful.

### 4.2 UK
Default label may be CV.

Rules:
- job-seeking CVs are often functionally equivalent to resumes;
- 1 page is often strong, but 1–2 pages may be acceptable when justified;
- tailoring intensity should be high;
- employer wording and competency language matter a lot;
- do not include sensitive personal details such as DOB, religion, gender, or marital status.

### 4.3 Switzerland / Europe (non-US)
Default label may be CV.

Rules:
- up to 2 pages may be acceptable;
- language skills and computer skills may be more prominent;
- some local contexts may tolerate references or “available on request”;
- if the destination is the US, revert to US resume rules.

### 4.4 Singapore / APAC fresh graduate
Default label: Resume.

Rules:
- 1 page by default;
- clean standard structure;
- consistent dates and formatting;
- co-curriculars, competitions, volunteering, projects, and certifications can carry meaningful signal if relevant.

## 5. Section-level guidance

### 5.1 Header
Include only decision-useful contact information.
Usually sufficient:
- name;
- email;
- phone;
- relevant link such as LinkedIn or portfolio.

Do not add unnecessary personal data.

### 5.2 Education
Keep concise but informative.

May include:
- institution;
- degree / major;
- expected graduation;
- GPA when it strengthens the application;
- relevant coursework when it signals role fit;
- awards or distinctions when strong.

For early-stage students, coursework may matter more. For later-stage students, remove weak or generic course lists.

### 5.3 Experience
This is usually the core section.

Each entry should make clear:
- organization or project;
- role;
- date range;
- location if relevant;
- concise bullets showing evidence.

Within a section, use reverse chronological order by default.

### 5.4 Projects / Research / Leadership
Use separate sections when they improve clarity and relevance.

Descriptive section headings can be better than generic headings if they help the recruiter immediately understand the signal.

### 5.5 Skills
Skills should be concrete and usable.

Good categories may include:
- programming / technical tools;
- data / analytical tools;
- lab methods;
- languages;
- software suites.

Do not fill this section with unsupported soft skills.

## 6. Bullet-writing rules

### 6.1 Default bullet formula
A strong bullet often follows this logic:
1. strong action verb;
2. task or context;
3. method, tool, scope, or counterpart when useful;
4. result, impact, scale, or reason.

Examples of acceptable result substitutes when metrics are unavailable:
- supported decision-making;
- informed recommendation;
- delivered final presentation or report;
- improved clarity, speed, organization, or usability;
- enabled the team to proceed or compare options.

### 6.2 Verb choices
Prefer strong, accurate verbs such as:
- analyzed;
- synthesized;
- evaluated;
- developed;
- built;
- designed;
- implemented;
- coordinated;
- presented.

Do not escalate support work into ownership verbs unless justified.

### 6.3 Density and length
Aim for high information density.

Typical target:
- English: around 18–28 words per bullet;
- Chinese: compact but clear, often around 25–50 characters.

Avoid overlong bullets that try to explain everything at once.

## 7. What to include conditionally

### GPA
Include when clearly favorable or conventionally useful.

### Coursework
Include when directly relevant or when the user is early in college and needs more evidence.

### High school
Include only for first-year students or when it provides unusual signal.

### Summary / Objective
Include only when highly specific and value-dense.
Do not include empty aspiration statements.

### Interests / Hobbies
Include only when relevant, distinctive, or useful for fit.
Generic filler should be removed.

### References
Usually exclude from the document unless local convention or the target region makes them appropriate.

## 8. What to cut first

When space is limited, cut in this order:
1. generic task bullets;
2. irrelevant coursework;
3. low-signal activities;
4. stale or weak experiences with little transfer value;
5. duplicate claims across sections.

Everything included should add value.

## 9. Formatting and scanability rules

Keep formatting:
- simple;
- consistent;
- easy to skim;
- low-risk for ATS.

Preferred formatting behavior:
- consistent date style;
- consistent bullet indentation;
- standard font size roughly 10–12 pt;
- reasonable whitespace;
- no clutter;
- no decorative design that hides information.

For US-style resumes especially, avoid:
- photos;
- graphics;
- text boxes;
- complex tables;
- overdesigned elements that reduce ATS readability.

## 10. Common failure modes

Avoid:
- pure responsibility lists;
- passive or vague openings;
- unsupported quantification;
- inflated ownership;
- buzzword stacking;
- filler content that does not support the role;
- inconsistent style across bullets;
- information that does not help the recruiter picture the candidate doing the work.

## 11. Role-family emphasis

### Consulting / strategy
Prioritize:
- structured analysis;
- research and synthesis;
- recommendation support;
- slide-writing or presentation output;
- problem-solving;
- stakeholder or team collaboration.

### Finance
Prioritize:
- analytical rigor;
- financial or market analysis;
- precision;
- due diligence or company research;
- concise, exact wording.

### Tech / product
Prioritize:
- building;
- implementation;
- experimentation;
- metrics;
- user or product reasoning;
- tools and technical execution.

### Research / academically adjacent roles
Prioritize:
- methods;
- data collection;
- literature review;
- experiment design;
- analysis;
- communication of findings.

## 12. Final quality bar

A strong finished resume should feel:
- tailored;
- evidence-driven;
- easy to scan;
- specific rather than general;
- active rather than passive;
- written to express, not impress;
- fact-based;
- professionally restrained.
